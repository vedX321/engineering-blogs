The STAT Pin That Wasn't Simply HIGH or LOW.

A datasheet investigation into charge-status outputs, high impedance, and the danger of assuming every digital-looking pin is a push-pull logic output.

HARDWARE / DEBUGGINGFIRST DRAFT · EDIT ME

Notebook note: This is a starter draft based on the investigation topic. Verify every technical claim against the exact MCP73832 datasheet revision and your circuit before publishing.

The question

While comparing single-cell battery-charger ICs, I started looking closely at the STAT output on the MCP73832. The important question wasn't just whether the pin indicates charging or charge completion. It was: what electrical state does the pin enter in each condition?

Why high impedance matters

A high-impedance output is not the same thing as a actively driven logic HIGH. In a high-impedance state, the IC is effectively disconnecting its output driver. The voltage measured at the pin can then depend on external components, leakage, and whatever else is connected to that node.

This is why reading a state table alone is not enough. Check the output type, the conditions attached to each state, and the recommended application circuit. An LED, pull-up resistor, microcontroller input, or another load can change what you observe.

What I need to verify

  • Confirm the STAT output structure and exact state table in the official datasheet.
  • Check whether the design needs an external pull-up or another biasing component.
  • Review how the chosen LED or MCU input affects the node in every operating state.
  • Measure the actual circuit and record the conditions, rather than relying only on a simulator.

The engineering lesson

When a pin's behavior seems confusing, don't immediately assume the IC is broken or the datasheet is wrong. Ask what the output stage is physically doing. “HIGH,” “LOW,” and “high impedance” describe different electrical situations.

Before publishing: add the exact datasheet revision, page/table reference, your schematic, measurements, and what you initially got wrong. That evidence is what turns a general explanation into a useful engineering case study.
